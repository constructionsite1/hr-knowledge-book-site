    1Principles behind pay

    Recall

    • Internal equity
    • External competitiveness
    • Individual equity
    • Equity theory (Adams, 1965)
    • Four groups of pay determinants

    Every pay decision must be defensible on two grounds. Internal equity means jobs of similar size are paid similarly within the organisation; it comes from job analysis, evaluation and grading. External competitiveness means pay is set at a deliberate level against the market; it comes from benchmarking and pay positioning. Individual equity means that differences in pay between people in the same job are explained by performance, experience or time in role, and it is managed through ranges and the merit matrix.

    The reason these principles matter is equity theory: people compare their contribution-to-reward ratio with others’, and perceived unfairness leads to lower effort, demands for more pay or exit.

    Pay is determined by four groups of factors acting at different stages: the job (size, accountability, criticality: sets the grade), the market (pay for matched jobs, skill scarcity: sets the range midpoint), the person (performance, experience: sets position in the range) and the organisation and law (ability to pay, philosophy, minimum wages, wage definition).

    2Job analysis and job evaluation

    Recall

    • JD vs job specification
    • Analysis methods
    • Ranking, classification, factor comparison, point-factor
    • Hay: Know-how, Problem solving, Accountability
    • Mercer IPE: Impact, Communication, Innovation, Knowledge, Risk
    • Limits of evaluation

    Job analysis collects systematic information about a job. Its outputs are the job description (purpose, responsibilities, scope, reporting) and the job specification (qualifications, experience, competencies). Methods include interviews, structured questionnaires, observation, expert workshops, work diaries and critical incidents. The principle is to analyse the job, not the jobholder, because jobs are stable and people change.

    Job evaluation ranks jobs by relative size within one organisation. It does not evaluate performance and does not set pay directly. It is needed because market data alone cannot produce a coherent structure.

    Method Key feature Use
    RankingWhole jobs placed in orderSmall firms; gives no measure of gaps
    ClassificationJobs slotted into pre-written grade descriptionsGovernment pay levels
    Factor comparisonBenchmark jobs ranked factor by factor with money valuesHistorical, rarely used
    Point-factorJobs scored on weighted factors; total points = sizeMost large companies

    The main licensed point-factor systems in India are the Korn Ferry Hay Guide Chart-Profile (Know-how, Problem solving as a percentage of know-how, Accountability) and Mercer IPE (Impact, Communication, Innovation, Knowledge, Risk; produces a position class that links directly to Mercer surveys). The main limitation is that factor weights are value judgements and can undervalue some types of work, such as care or customer-facing roles.

    How is job analysis different from job evaluation?

    Show answer

    Analysis describes a job and produces the JD and specification. Evaluation uses that information to judge the job’s size relative to other jobs and produces a ranking or points score used for grading.

    3Grades and benchmarking

    Recall

    • Grade = band of points
    • Grade vs title vs level
    • Broadbanding
    • Survey houses
    • Match on content
    • P25, P50, P75
    • Lead, match, lag, segment

    A grade groups jobs of similar evaluated size so they share one pay range. Grades differ from titles (which describe the work) and levels (which describe progression in a job family). Many narrow grades give control but bureaucracy; broadbanding (4–6 wide bands) gives flexibility for lateral moves but weaker cost control.

    Benchmarking compares pay for matched jobs with the external market. Reliable data comes from salary surveys run by Aon, Mercer, WTW and Korn Ferry, in which participating companies submit job-matched data. Job portals and review sites give trend signals but unverified data. Jobs are matched on content, not title. Survey results are read as percentiles: P25, the median (P50) and P75. Data is aged to a common date, and the comparison market is the set of employers competing for the same talent.

    Pay positioning is the decision on where to stand: lead (P65–P75) for scarce or critical skills, match (P50) for most roles, lag (below P50) when the non-cash offer is strong. Most organisations use a segmented strategy.

    4Pay ranges and individual positioning

    Recall

    • Min, mid, max
    • Spread = (max − min) ÷ min
    • Min = mid ÷ (1 + spread/2)
    • Midpoint progression; overlap
    • Compa-ratio
    • Range penetration
    • Red and green circles
    • Pay compression

    Each grade has a pay range. The midpoint is the pay for a fully competent jobholder, set from market data at the chosen position. The minimum is for people new to the job and the maximum is the ceiling for the job. A range exists because people in the same job differ in proficiency. Spread is typically 30–40% for junior grades, 40–60% for managerial and 60%+ for executive grades. Midpoint progression between grades is typically 8–15% at junior levels and 15–25% at senior levels.

    Example: midpoint ₹8.4 lakh, spread 50% gives a minimum of 8.4 ÷ 1.25 = ₹6.72 lakh and a maximum of 6.72 × 1.5 = ₹10.08 lakh.

    Compa-ratio = salary ÷ range midpoint × 100. Around 100 means paid at target; below 90 means new or underpaid; above 110 means very experienced or high for the job. Range penetration = (salary − min) ÷ (max − min) × 100 shows how far through the range a salary sits.

    People above the maximum are red circles: no pay cut, but smaller or no fixed increases and one-time payments instead. People below the minimum are green circles and are brought up to it. Pay compression occurs when market pay rises faster than internal increments, so new hires earn as much as experienced staff; it is fixed through targeted market corrections.

    CorrectionThe compendium defines compa-ratio against the market average. The standard definition uses the midpoint of the employee’s own range.

    Salary ₹18 lakh, range ₹16–24 lakh, midpoint ₹20 lakh. Compa-ratio and range penetration?

    Show answer

    Compa-ratio = 18 ÷ 20 × 100 = 90. Range penetration = (18 − 16) ÷ (24 − 16) × 100 = 25%.

    5Increments

    Recall

    • Increment budget (~9% in India)
    • Merit matrix: rating × compa-ratio
    • Promotion increase
    • Lump sum
    • Market adjustment

    Each year an overall increment budget is set from market movement, inflation and business performance. Aon reported an average increase of 8.9% in India in 2025 and projected 9.1% for 2026. The budget is distributed through a merit matrix that combines the performance rating with compa-ratio: for the same rating, people lower in the range receive a larger percentage, so strong performers move towards the midpoint and salaries above it grow more slowly. Promotion increases are separate and usually bring the person at least to the new grade’s minimum. Lump-sum payments reward high-range performers without raising fixed pay. Market adjustments correct compression off-cycle.

    Two employees both rated outstanding: compa-ratios 85 and 115. Who gets the higher increase?

    Show answer

    The first, because they are paid well below the target for the job. The second may get a smaller fixed increase plus a one-time bonus, so performance is rewarded without pushing fixed pay further above the job’s value.

    6CTC, components and tax

    Recall

    • CTC, gross, net
    • Basic, DA, HRA, LTA, special allowance
    • New vs old tax regime
    • ₹75,000 standard deduction
    • Nil tax to ₹12 lakh taxable
    • Income-tax Act, 2025 (1 April 2026)
    • Employer NPS 14%

    CTC is the employer’s total annual cost: gross salary plus employer PF, gratuity provision, target variable pay and employer-paid benefits. Gross is salary before employee deductions. Net is gross minus employee PF, professional tax, TDS and other deductions. Offers should be compared on guaranteed cash, realistic variable pay and benefit value, not CTC alone. A ₹18 lakh CTC with ₹15 lakh gross gives about ₹1.14 lakh a month in hand under the new regime.

    Salaries are split into components because different laws use different bases. HRA and LTA exemptions apply only in the old tax regime. The new regime is the default: nil tax on taxable income up to ₹12 lakh after rebate, a ₹75,000 standard deduction for salaried employees, and slabs rising to 30% above ₹24 lakh. Employer NPS contributions remain deductible up to 14% of basic + DA in the new regime. The Income-tax Act, 2025 replaced the 1961 Act from 1 April 2026; refer to provisions by name. Because most employees use the new regime, tax-driven salary structuring and flexible benefit plans have lost much of their value.

    7Wages under the Code on Wages

    Recall

    • Wages = basic + DA + retaining allowance
    • 50% add-back
    • Effects on gratuity, PF, take-home
    • Not “basic = 50% of CTC”

    From 21 November 2025, wages means basic pay, DA and retaining allowance. HRA, conveyance, statutory bonus, overtime, commission, employer PF and gratuity are excluded, but if excluded items exceed 50% of total remuneration, the excess is added back to wages. The rule stops employers from splitting pay into allowances to reduce statutory dues.

    Example: total ₹1,00,000 with basic ₹30,000 and allowances ₹70,000. Excess over 50% is ₹20,000, so wages for PF, gratuity and bonus are ₹50,000.

    Effects: higher gratuity liability, higher PF where paid on actual wages, slightly lower take-home pay, and widespread CTC restructuring to keep basic + DA near half of total remuneration. The law caps exclusions; it does not literally require basic to be 50% of CTC.

    Gratuity for 9 years 7 months of service and last wages of ₹45,000?

    Show answer

    Service rounds up to 10 years. 15 × 45,000 × 10 ÷ 26 = ₹2,59,615.

    PF on wages of ₹25,000: how is the employer’s 12% split?

    Show answer

    ₹3,000 in total: ₹2,083 to EPS (8.33%) and ₹917 to EPF, plus EDLI and administration charges of 0.5% each (₹125 each).

    9Philosophy and pay mix

    Recall

    • Compensation philosophy
    • Six design decisions
    • Pay mix by level
    • Netflix, Google, Goldman, Tata, start-ups

    A compensation philosophy is a leadership-approved statement of how pay supports strategy. It answers six decisions: market positioning, internal equity, pay mix, pay for performance, structure and transparency. (The compendium calls these the “6Ps”; it is a teaching device, and “Pay Poisoning” is a typo for pay positioning.)

    Pay mix shifts towards variable and long-term pay with seniority because senior roles influence results more: unionised workmen are almost all fixed, officers 85–95% fixed, senior leaders in listed companies 50–65% fixed. Examples: Netflix pays top of market with high fixed pay; Google combines base, bonus and large RSUs; Goldman Sachs is bonus-heavy; many Tata companies match the market and emphasise stability; start-ups conserve cash and offer ESOPs.

    10Variable pay and long-term incentives

    Recall

    • STI types
    • Target %, metrics, payout curve
    • Threshold, target, maximum
    • Pool × individual modifier
    • Expectancy theory conditions
    • ESOP, RSU, PSU, SAR, phantom
    • Vesting, cliff, exercise
    • SEBI SBEB Regulations 2021

    Short-term incentives reward results over a year or less: annual bonus, sales commission, productivity-linked incentives or gainsharing in plants, profit sharing and spot awards. A bonus plan defines eligibility, a target incentive (e.g., 8–15% of salary for managers), 3–5 metrics with weights, a payout curve (threshold, target, maximum), a funding pool and an individual modifier. Example: target ₹2.4 lakh × pool funding 90% × individual multiplier 1.2 = ₹2.59 lakh.

    Variable pay motivates when employees can influence the metric, payout reliably follows achievement and the amount is meaningful (Vroom’s expectancy theory). It fails when linked to results employees can’t influence, a common criticism of company-linked variable pay for junior staff in Indian IT services.

    Long-term incentives retain and align people over 3–4 years. An ESOP is a right to buy shares at a fixed exercise price and is worthless if the price falls below it. An RSU delivers shares on vesting and always has some value. Performance shares vest only if targets are met. SARs and phantom stock pay the value or growth in cash or shares without issuing real equity. ESOPs are taxed as a perquisite at exercise and as capital gains on sale. Listed-company schemes follow the SEBI (SBEB & SE) Regulations, 2021. Start-ups prefer ESOPs; mature listed companies prefer RSUs.

    Is variable pay a good motivator?

    Show answer

    Only when employees can influence the metric, payouts reliably follow achievement and the amount is meaningful. Otherwise it acts as profit sharing or becomes an expected part of pay.

    11Benefits and total rewards

    Recall

    • Statutory vs voluntary benefits
    • GMC, GTL, GPA, NPS
    • Flexible benefits
    • Total reward statement
    • WorldatWork: compensation, benefits, well-being, recognition, development

    Statutory benefits are PF, EPS and EDLI, ESI, gratuity, maternity benefit (26 weeks) and crèche at 50+ employees, employee compensation for injury, statutory bonus and leave under state laws. Voluntary benefits commonly include group medical cover (₹3–10 lakh family floater), group term life (often 3× pay), personal accident cover, employer NPS, extra leave, paternity and adoption leave, employee assistance programmes and, in plants, canteen, transport and housing. Flexible benefits let employees choose within a budget; their purpose has shifted from tax saving to choice. Total reward statements show employees the full value of their package.

    The total rewards model (WorldatWork) integrates compensation, benefits, well-being, recognition and development. It matters most when cash cannot be the main differentiator: ITC uses cross-business careers, and a remote plant competes through housing, schools and safety.

    12Pay equity, transparency and governance

    Recall

    • Raw vs adjusted gap
    • Regression method
    • Code on Wages: equal remuneration
    • BRSR; director-to-median ratio
    • EU directive (June 2026)
    • NRC; 11% managerial limit

    The raw gender pay gap compares median pay of men and women overall and mainly reflects representation in senior grades. The adjusted gap controls for grade, role, experience, performance and location, usually by regressing log salary on these and a gender variable; it indicates unexplained differences in pay decisions. Unexplained gaps often come from starting salaries anchored on previous pay, negotiation differences, slower progression after maternity leave and evaluation schemes that undervalue some work.

    The Code on Wages bars gender discrimination (including transgender identity) in wages and recruitment. Listed companies disclose wages paid to women under SEBI’s BRSR and the director-to-median pay ratio under the Companies Act. The EU Pay Transparency Directive (national laws due by June 2026) requires pay ranges in job ads and gap reporting, affecting multinationals. The common middle path on transparency is to publish ranges by grade. Executive pay is overseen by the board’s Nomination and Remuneration Committee, and total managerial remuneration in public companies is limited to 11% of net profits without further approval.

    Raw gender gap 18%, adjusted gap 3%. What does it mean and what would you do?

    Show answer

    Most of the gap reflects fewer women in senior grades. Correct the 3% through a dedicated budget, then focus on the pipeline: hiring into senior roles, promotion rates and progression after maternity leave, and stop anchoring offers on previous salary.
