    Engagement, Well-being, EX and EVP: Revision Notes

    Chapter 5 · Notes version of the book chapter

    Condensed notes for revision. Cover the right side and explain each cue in the left column; fold the page at the dashed line to test yourself.

    1Concepts

    Recall

    • Engagement (Kahn 1990; Schaufeli: vigour, dedication, absorption)
    • Satisfaction
    • Commitment: affective, continuance, normative
    • EX
    • Well-being
    • Gallup 2025: 21%

    Engagement is active commitment shown in discretionary effort. Kahn (1990) described people bringing themselves physically, cognitively and emotionally to work; Schaufeli defined work engagement as vigour, dedication and absorption. Satisfaction is passive contentment; a satisfied employee can be disengaged. Commitment is attachment to the organisation: affective (want to stay), continuance (need to stay) and normative (feel obliged to stay), per Meyer and Allen. Employee experience is the sum of interactions across the lifecycle. Well-being is physical, mental, financial and social health. Herzberg explains the difference: hygiene factors prevent dissatisfaction, motivators create engagement.

    Gallup’s meta-analyses link top-quartile engagement to better profitability, productivity, safety, absenteeism and retention (correlation, with causality both ways). Gallup’s 2025 report found 21% of employees worldwide engaged, down from 23%, with manager engagement falling most.

    2Models

    Recall

    • Aon: Say, Stay, Strive (6 items)
    • Gallup Q12 hierarchy
    • Manager ≈ 70% of team variance
    • Kahn: meaningfulness, safety, availability
    • JD-R (Bakker & Demerouti)

    Aon defines engagement by outcomes: employees Say positive things, want to Stay and Strive with extra effort, measured by six items and linked to drivers (work, people, opportunities, rewards, company practices, quality of life). Gallup Q12 describes workplace conditions in a hierarchy: basic needs (knowing what is expected, having materials), individual contribution, teamwork and growth. Most items are under the manager’s control, so Gallup attributes about 70% of variance in team engagement to the manager. Kahn named three conditions: meaningfulness, psychological safety and availability. The JD-R model says high demands cause burnout and resources (autonomy, support, feedback, growth) create engagement and buffer demands, so both are managed by reducing excess demands and adding resources.

    3Drivers

    Recall

    • Manager
    • Meaningful work (JCM)
    • Growth
    • Recognition
    • Fairness
    • Leadership, purpose
    • Well-being
    • Perks are not drivers

    The strongest drivers are the immediate manager, meaningful work (skill variety, task identity, task significance, autonomy and feedback from Hackman and Oldham), growth and career, recognition, fairness in pay, ratings and treatment (equity theory), confidence in leadership and purpose, and well-being and flexibility. Perks such as free food or offsites raise satisfaction briefly but rarely appear among top drivers; leading with them in an interview signals a shallow answer.

    4Measurement and action

    Recall

    • Census, pulse, lifecycle surveys
    • eNPS formula
    • Stay vs exit interviews
    • Passive data, AI listening
    • Anonymity threshold
    • Driver analysis: high impact, low score
    • Act in 4–6 weeks

    Organisations combine an annual census survey (with driver analysis and benchmarks), pulse surveys, lifecycle surveys at moments such as 30 and 90 days after joining, stay interviews with valued employees, exit surveys (more candid a few months after exit), passive data such as absenteeism and overtime, and AI listening tools such as Amber by inFeedo.

    eNPS asks how likely employees are to recommend the organisation as a place to work (0–10): % promoters (9–10) − % detractors (0–6), ranging from −100 to +100. Example: 35% promoters and 25% detractors give +10. Results are shown only above an anonymity threshold (often 5–10 respondents), and priorities are drivers with high impact and low scores.

    Action process: share results within four to six weeks, pick two or three priorities per team, let managers own team plans with HR support, communicate “you said, we did”, and re-measure with pulses. Surveys fail mainly when no visible action follows.

    200 responses: 70 score 9–10, 80 score 7–8, 50 score 0–6. eNPS?

    Show answer

    35% − 25% = +10.

    5Attrition and retention

    Recall

    • Separations ÷ average headcount
    • Voluntary, involuntary
    • Regretted
    • Early attrition (cohort)
    • India 17.7% (2024)
    • Cost components
    • Diagnose by segment

    Annual attrition = separations ÷ average headcount × 100; monthly rates × 12 approximate an annual rate. Always split voluntary from involuntary, regretted from non-regretted, and measure early attrition as hires leaving within 90 or 365 days ÷ hires in the cohort. Aon reported Indian attrition of 17.7% in 2024 (18.7% in 2023); frontline roles often exceed 50%.

    Turnover costs include separation, vacancy, replacement and ramp-up. Diagnose by manager, tenure, pay position (compa-ratios of leavers), exit reasons and shift or commute factors, then use the matching lever: manager coaching, onboarding fixes, market correction, career paths, flexibility or retention incentives.

    High engagement scores but high attrition: why?

    Show answer

    Leavers didn’t respond or aren’t surveyed (contract or new staff), pay is uncompetitive, or the survey measures satisfaction rather than intent to stay. Compare by segment with exit and pay data.

    6Different workforces

    Recall

    • Shop floor drivers
    • Union involvement
    • Frontline: first 30 days
    • Gig: transparency, deactivation
    • Hybrid: proximity bias

    On the shop floor, drivers are safety, fair supervisors, fair wage settlements, predictable shifts, canteen, transport and housing, family welfare and skill growth; listen in local languages and face to face, and involve the union. Corporate staff value growth, managers, meaningful work and flexibility. Frontline staff need reliable pay, safe shifts, respectful supervisors and fast onboarding, with the first 30 days critical. Gig workers need earnings stability, transparent ratings and fair deactivation. Hybrid teams need clear expectations and protection against proximity bias.

    7Well-being and burnout

    Recall

    • Physical, mental, financial, social
    • Burnout: WHO ICD-11
    • Exhaustion, cynicism, reduced efficacy
    • Organisation > team > individual
    • EY Pune 2024
    • Mental Healthcare Act 2017
    • Right to disconnect: not law
    • Karnataka menstrual leave

    Well-being has physical, mental, financial and social dimensions. The WHO’s ICD-11 describes burnout as an occupational phenomenon from chronic, unmanaged workplace stress, with exhaustion, cynicism and reduced efficacy. The most effective interventions are organisational (workload, staffing, deadlines, manager training, flexibility), then team norms, then individual support such as counselling.

    India: the EY Pune employee’s death in 2024 and the 70- and 90-hour week remarks made working hours a national debate; the OSH Code limits the week to 48 hours with overtime at 2× and a cap of 144 hours a quarter. The Mental Healthcare Act, 2017 requires insurance parity for mental illness. A right to disconnect is law in France, Portugal and Australia but exists in India only as private member’s bills. Karnataka (2025) grants one paid day of menstrual leave a month.

    8Employee experience

    Recall

    • Lifecycle stages
    • Personas
    • Journey maps
    • Moments that matter
    • AI in EX: benefits, risks

    EX applies customer-experience design to the lifecycle (attract, recruit, onboard, develop, perform, retain, separate, alumni). Personas represent groups with distinct needs, journey maps show steps and friction, and moments that matter (first day, first one-to-one, missed promotion, return from leave, resignation) shape perception most. Typical fixes target day-one access, slow reimbursements and unprepared managers. AI assistants and attrition prediction help but bring bias, surveillance and human-contact risks.

    9EVP

    Recall

    • EVP = offer in return for contribution
    • Gartner: Opportunity, People, Organisation, Work, Rewards
    • EVP internal, brand external
    • Five build steps
    • Segment
    • HCLTech, Tata Steel

    The EVP is what the organisation offers in return for employees’ contribution; the employer brand is how the market perceives it. Gartner groups EVP attributes into Opportunity, People, Organisation, Work and Rewards. Build an EVP by researching employees and competitors, identifying what is true and distinctive, segmenting it for key groups, launching it internally first and measuring offer acceptance, applicant quality, referrals and attrition. HCLTech’s “Employees First” is the classic EVP built on a management philosophy; Tata Steel pioneered the 8-hour day (1912), paid leave (1920) and maternity benefit (1928).

    EVP for a quick-commerce company?

    Show answer

    Segment it: frontline staff value reliable pay, safe shifts, respectful supervisors, fast onboarding and progression; technology hires value problem scale, ownership, pace and equity. One brand, different promises.
